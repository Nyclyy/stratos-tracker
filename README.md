# Stratos

A lightweight flight-simulation companion: live map, public ADS-B traffic, airports and charts, a career logbook, flight replay, landing analytics, route planning, weather briefings, and a secure phone dashboard.

Stratos runs as a static website/PWA, an Electron desktop app, and an optional local Python simulator bridge. It is designed to be useful without a simulator, and explicit about what is live, estimated, or unavailable.

| Integrated charts (ILS/LOC free, the rest Premium) | The current FAA d-TPP catalogue per airport (diagrams, SIDs, STARs, approaches, minimums, hot spots) in a source-agnostic provider registry, a bundled pdf.js viewer with zoom, pan, fit, reset, pages, previous/next document, full screen and a document cache, and a trusted Premium gate. **ILS and LOC approach charts open for everyone**; the other categories need a verified licence. Search accepts an ICAO code or an airport name, every row is labelled Free or Premium, the airport header names the airport, country and available categories, and the last airport/chart is restored on return. Coverage is the United States and its territories; Canada, Europe and Australia show publisher links only (no embedded or mirrored charts), and **Open PDF…** displays a chart you downloaded from that publisher locally, without uploading or caching it |
| Route vertical profile | Planned altitude, climb/cruise/descent, TOC/TOD and per-waypoint inspection from the same estimate the planner reports; imported flight-plan altitudes are kept as constraints. TOC/TOD are interpolated **inside the route leg they belong to** by a pure profile module: the plan's own altitudes end the climb and start the descent, a measured (or default) gradient fills the gaps, and a gradient-derived marker is marked `≈` so an estimate never reads as a planned figure |
| Route weather along the plan | NOAA METAR at up to eight airports on the route with surface wind components against the flown course and honest IFR/gust/crosswind notes; real-world only, not winds aloft or simulator weather |
| Offline vector world map | Working; the **World** style draws bundled Natural Earth country outlines with muted country names, letter-spaced continent names and italic ocean names, adds seas only when zoomed past a whole-world view, and needs no tiles, key or network |
| Globe on the Flight Deck map | The tracker's map is the **same globe** as the Flight Planner's, not a lookalike: one shared engine (`js/lib/globeview.js`) projects every layer, and the **Projection** switch in the map's Layers sheet turns the flat chart into a movable orthographic sphere that spins under drag from anywhere on the surface, zooms toward the pointer and pinch, and frames the route on *Fit* (turning the globe to the route's own vector mean centre rather than to a fixed latitude). The same four surfaces are offered — **Outlines** (the Natural Earth coastlines the tracker already bundles, the default and the only one that makes no request), **Imagery** (NASA Blue Marble through NASA GIBS), **Relief** and **Elevation** (shaded and hypsometric, from the real AWS/Mapzen terrain tiles) — with **Reset view**, an explicit **Read terrain** action, and the publisher named on the sphere and in the map's own attribution line. The notes under the surface buttons say what the sphere is drawn from, what is still loading, and — after a terrain reading — the ground at the centre with the level and ground resolution it was read at and how many samples inside the radius had data, in the module's own words rather than the page's. Route lines are folded onto the limb where a leg crosses the horizon; navigation symbols, labels and hit-tests ask whether a point is on the near side, so nothing on the far hemisphere is drawn or taps through, and the flat chart's pan and zoom integration is switched off while the sphere owns the camera. Labels are placed by pixels per degree instead of a Web Mercator tile level, the graticule is not drawn over imagery, and a georeferenced approach chart still opens flat — leaving the globe and returning to it, route reframed, when the chart closes. The raster is built from this page's own draw, at a level chosen from the sphere and rebuilt only when the camera, surface or loaded tiles change; the choice of projection and surface is remembered. The globe is one option on the existing map, so live tracking, traffic, the flight plan, the logbook and the recorded trail are unaffected |

| Career dashboard, records, milestones, drill-down | Working, with customizable tiles |
| Interactive route planner | The planned route is drawn on the Flight Deck map itself as soon as it exists — fitted to the route, labelled, with a **Route** layer toggle and two-way sync with the planner. The planner page keeps its own editing map with a collapsible plan sidebar and route header; selectable legs and waypoints; add, delete, reorder and drag waypoints; undo; a leg inspector with geodesic distance, initial true course, magnetic course only when variation is supplied, ETE at the planned/live groundspeed, leg position and endpoint coordinates; total distance/legs/ETE and live remaining distance. PLN/FMS/GPX import/export and SimBrief import stay on the same route |
| Aircraft checklists | Ten ToLiss/FlightFactor profiles plus a generic checklist, including a dedicated A340-600 flow; aircraft detection from telemetry with manual override; phase inference from groundspeed/altitude/vertical speed; per-flight progress, compact/full modes, custom items, import/export and reset; optional phase callouts off by default; **community simulator aid, not a verified aircraft manual** |
| Phone pairing on the local network | **One tap, no QR and no URL.** The bridge answers multicast DNS for `stratos.local`, so the phone opens `http://stratos.local:8765/pair/connect`, enrolls itself and lands on the live dashboard; telemetry then reconnects on its own after a Wi-Fi change or bridge restart — the map also treats twelve seconds of silence as a dead socket and reopens the stream, because a restarted bridge does not always close the old one. The QR/six-digit code remains as a fallback for networks that block multicast. The hosted page never navigates on its own: a phone whose PC is switched off keeps the whole Flight Deck and is offered one tap to the PC's map, so no visit ends on the browser's error page. Every phone gets its own HttpOnly secret, remembered across visits; the PC lists paired phones and can forget one or all; revoked streams close within ~5 s |
| Flight Deck shell | Five top-level destinations everywhere — Flight Deck, Flight Planning, Logbook, Explore and Profile — in one grouped rail that opens icon-only, expands to a labelled sidebar, names each icon on hover and keyboard focus, and shares one keyboard ring with the bar; the pages without a rail carry the same five in the bar (and on a second bar row on phones), while Logbook, Weather and Live use the same five in their bottom bar. Charts, live tracking, weather and the data pack stay one tap away in the map's own tools menu, and Weather, Airports, Charts, Statistics and Live are linked from Explore. A top bar with the wordmark and its live state line, one centred search, Support Us / Go Premium and the UTC clock, and a full-bleed map: the flight card floats over it with real telemetry and a corner badge names the place and the live aircraft count |
| Flight-plan card | Replaces the old pencil-in-a-circle button. Before a plan it is an import prompt; after one it is a compact route card — callsign, aircraft, departure and arrival, SID and STAR each with the planned runway directly underneath, cruise, distance, time and departure/arrival weather categories — that expands in place to route, alternate, raw METARs with their age, weights, fuel, passengers, cargo and live progress, with Edit route and Flight summary one tap away. Its preference is remembered, it never covers the telemetry card, and unavailable values say so instead of being filled in |
| Aviation colour board | Settings documents the one palette the interface uses, with a swatch, HEX and job for each role — signature cyan, navigation violet, aviation amber, instrument white, deep charcoal, muted grey, alert red and confirmed green — and real controls, a selected-waypoint marker, the route line, a TOD tag and the live dot shown beside it |
| Flight overview dashboard | Reports the simulator link, the live flight, the saved route pair, planned cruise level, distance, ETE and airborne progress, and admits a value it does not have instead of inventing one |
| Top of descent from your own descents | Working; the bridge measures the real gradient of each completed flight and the profile uses it, falling back to 200 ft/nm until one exists |
| Route symbols, labels and selection | One chart symbol per meaning (en-route fix, airway fix, SID, STAR, airport, VOR, NDB, unclassified navaid, TOC/TOD), long legs carry their distance and heading, labels give way instead of overprinting, and a click or tap selects a point with a purple halo and thicker glyph. Selection is a drawing layer only: the waypoint-to-waypoint line is byte-for-byte unchanged. A plan imported in the Route Planner keeps its SID/STAR tags, its airways and its stored SimBrief plan, so the same symbols and the same procedures appear on the map and in the tracking card |
| Terminal procedure geometry | SID and STAR runway transitions follow real published legs, from the runway threshold to the procedure's terminal fix, in either of two providers. The bridge reads X-Plane 12's CIFP folder and resolves each fix against the simulator's own fix, navaid, airport and runway records. When the simulator has no data for the airport — MSFS, or no X-Plane installed — the dataset Stratos ships (`navdata/cifp`, the FAA's public-domain CIFP, US airports, rebuilt by `scripts/build-navdata.py`) supplies the same geometry. Only the plan's own SID/STAR and runway are used (including a combined "both runways" transition, labelled as such), and only the legs the plan does not already draw are painted, under the unchanged waypoint line. Procedures the source publishes without any runway leg — most FAA STARs, which carry a common trunk plus en-route transitions — are drawn from their verified trunk instead of being dropped, and the runway is never joined to them by a straight line. A leg the data cannot place is counted and reported, never guessed; without that data no path is drawn and the card says why. The AIRAC cycle and provider are shown |
| Flight trail and aircraft card | The bridge records the flight from its first movement; the map and the phone draw that recorded path rather than starting at the moment the page opened. A record that starts where the drawn trail stopped — the rollout after a landing — continues that line instead of erasing the flight behind it, and a flight that starts anywhere else replaces it. Selecting **any** aircraft — your own or a VATSIM/ADS-B target you click — draws that aircraft's own recorded path, not only yours. Positions are recorded for every aircraft in view on each traffic refresh and kept per aircraft id; the page batches them to the bridge, which keeps them for 45 minutes in `traffic_trails.json`, so the path survives a page reload, a bridge restart and a browser refresh, and is served back to anything the bridge hosts. A trail is always what Stratos actually observed — no history is invented. On both maps — the Flight Deck and the phone's small live map — the path is one continuous line coloured by **height above ground** from one shared ramp: white while taxiing and on the runway, then blue through green and yellow to orange and magenta at the flight levels, so the climb and the descent are readable at a glance. The recording carries AGL per sample; a track without it is coloured by height above its lowest point, and a route preview with no altitudes keeps the plain accent line. Traffic feeds publish only a current position, so a target's trail starts when Stratos first sees it and the card says how much has been recorded. The phone's own live map draws that nearby traffic too — the VATSIM pilots it can read itself plus the aircraft the bridge has already polled — with a **Traffic** layer button; tapping a target selects it, reports only the fields it actually has (type, filed route, altitude, speed, heading, distance) and draws that aircraft's path from the same bridge store, so the trail on the phone matches the one on the PC and survives a reload. A phone paired through the relay instead (on a different network) is served by the relay: the bridge posts the same traffic list and the new trail samples to `POST /api/traffic` every 15 s, and the phone reads them with its read token, so the hosted page shows the desktop's traffic and the same recorded paths rather than a blank map. The aircraft card expands to photo, registration, type, phase, planned cruise and runways, and minimizes to a live strip plus SID/STAR with the departure runway under the SID and the arrival runway under the STAR |
| Stratos EFB (second product, `app/efb.html`) | An iPad-style flight bag beside the tracker: a home screen of app icons over a dark instrument wallpaper, a live status bar (connection, UTC, plan), a dock and a home control, all reachable from the Flight Deck rail and the bar on every page. The decorative bezel is desktop chrome only — on a real tablet, any touch screen or a narrow window the screen is the viewport, so an iPad never shows a tablet drawn inside itself. It is its own installable app: `manifest-efb.webmanifest` puts **Stratos EFB** on the home screen with a distinct id from the tracker, launches it standalone on the bag, and the shell worker returns the bag (not the Flight Deck) when it opens offline; a shortcut can jump straight to Briefing, Charts or Live. Eleven apps, each with its own layout: **Briefing** (the stored SimBrief OFP — identity, procedures and runways, times, weights, fuel, route and the published PDF — with every value labelled OFP and missing values named), **Charts** (the same FAA d-TPP catalogue, viewer, validity window and Premium gate as the tracker), **Weather** (NOAA METAR/TAF decoded locally, runway wind components, an explicit no-SIGMET-feed state), **Performance** (pressure/density altitude, ISA deviation and wind components computed exactly; V-speeds and field lengths only from a validated dataset you import, interpolated inside its own axes and never extrapolated — with no dataset it says so instead of inventing numbers), **Weight & Balance** (SimBrief weights or a manual load, limits only when the type's data is present, CG only when arms/moments are supplied), **Fuel** (categories kept separate and unit-safe, each value labelled plan or entered), **Documents** (the community checklists and aircraft limitations, with progress), **Airports** (runways, frequencies, approaches from the airport database; no invented NOTAMs), **Live Flight** (the existing bridge telemetry on a map with the recorded path, next waypoint and progress), **Logbook** (the same local logbook, no second store) and **Settings** (aircraft, units, wallpaper, refresh, cache, sources) |
| Flight Planner chart: real navigation points, 40 NM query, globe | The Flight Planner's chart carries the real navigation points around it, not a decorative background. The centre of the view — never the aircraft's position — is the centre of a **great-circle query** (20/40/80/160/320 NM, 40 NM by default, so a continent's worth of points can be seen at once), and every record inside it is drawn with its chart symbol and labelled by collision-free placement. Airports and radio navaids come from OurAirports (CC0, the bridge's worldwide list or the bundled snapshot); en-route **fixes and the simulator's own navaids come only from the simulator's navigation data** through `/navdata/near`, which is read in place on the pilot's PC and never bundled or redistributed. A tap resolves to the published record nearest it, offers a choice when several are within reach, and — where nothing published exists within the tap radius — offers the exact position as a **custom coordinate** labelled as one (47°27N 122°19W, never a generated `W01`). The readout states the chart centre, the radius with the number of records inside it, the visible span in NM, the selected point with its distance from the centre, and the altitudes that are actually known — the aircraft's, live from the simulator, the nearest airport's elevation, and, on the globe, the ground's own elevation from the terrain tiles — each with its source; nothing else is filled in. The radius circle is **off** unless the pilot turns it on: the radius decides which points are drawn, and a circle across the chart is not needed to read that. **Globe** switches to a movable orthographic globe sharing the same route, points and query: it turns under drag, zooms toward the pointer, frames the route on *Fit*, and offers four surfaces — **Outlines** (the Natural Earth 1:110m coastlines already bundled for the tracker, drawn by default and the only surface that makes no request), **Imagery** (NASA Blue Marble through NASA GIBS), **Relief** and **Elevation** (shaded and hypsometric, both from the real AWS/Mapzen terrain tiles and distinct from each other) — with a camera reset back to the route, an explicit **Read terrain** action, and the publisher named on the sphere and in the readout. Elevations are read at a deeper level than the surface is drawn at (about 230 m per pixel against about 15 km per pixel here) and reported with the level, the ground resolution and how many samples had data, because those tiles are a display product: ETOPO1 states it is not suitable for navigation, and Stratos uses none of it for terrain clearance. Switching projection keeps the place in view and the plan untouched, the ACROSS/scale readout follows the zoom in both modes, and a globe that is not asked for tiles builds no decoder and fetches nothing. **Reset plan** clears the departure, route, procedures, fuel and alternate, removes the route the Navigator and the Flight Deck read, and keeps the pilot's saved plans |

## Quick start

- Website: open `index.html`, then `app/`. The flight bag is `app/efb.html` (or **Stratos EFB** in the Flight Deck rail).
- Local development: `npm run dev` serves `app/` on http://127.0.0.1:8123 and proxies live ADS-B traffic, which browsers cannot fetch directly (the mirrors send no CORS headers). A plain static server can still serve the files, but live ADS-B then reports why it is unavailable.
- Windows: download `Stratos-Setup.exe` from the latest GitHub release, or build with `npm ci && npm run dist:win`.
- Simulator bridge: `python3 stratos_bridge.py` (the desktop app starts it automatically).
- Tests: `npm test`, `npm run test:py`, `npm run test:integration`.

No API keys are required for the app. The optional relay is a small Node server in `relay/`; see the deployment guide.

Running from source (`npm start`) reports a labelled development entitlement, so the Premium surfaces can be tested without a licence service. A packaged build needs `STRATOS_DEV_PREMIUM=1` or a configured `STRATOS_ENTITLEMENT_URL`.

## Honest limitations

SID and STAR paths are drawn from the fixes the imported plan lists, plus the procedure geometry the bridge can read: X-Plane 12's own CIFP on that PC, or — when the simulator has none for the airport, as with MSFS, whose navdata is proprietary and unread — the FAA dataset Stratos ships in `navdata/cifp` for United States airports. Most ARINC 424 files publish no runway leg at all, only a common trunk plus en-route transitions; those verified legs are drawn, the runway is not joined to them by an invented line, and the tracking card says so. Where neither provider has the procedure, nothing is drawn, the plan's own fixes still appear as the plan lists them, and the card says which plan a SID and STAR came from and why a path is missing.

The globe — on the Flight Planner's chart and on the Flight Deck map, which run the same engine — reads its imagery and terrain surfaces from two public services at runtime, and both were exercised against them: the NASA GIBS pyramid (whose level widths are read from the service's own capabilities, not assumed) and the AWS/Mapzen terrain tiles, verified against known ground — Denver, the Dead Sea, Seattle, Denali and Everest all read within the accuracy the tile level allows, and the level and sample counts are stated rather than implied. Neither service is mirrored, proxied or bundled, and the globe draws bundled outlines when they are unreachable. Terrain elevation and imagery remain a display product for a simulator map, never a navigation source.

The route planner, checklist engine, one-tap LAN pairing (mDNS name, auto-enrollment and reconnect) and the MSFS connector were exercised on Windows 11 with Chromium and the real Python bridge. X-Plane telemetry (including the UDP dataref identity read) and a real iPhone/Safari session or production relay host were not available, so those paths are implemented and source-tested but still need a flight on your hardware. Checklist content is community-compiled from public procedures and is deliberately labelled unverified; it does not replace the aircraft add-on's own checklists or manual. The bridge listens on the LAN by default for pairing, protected by the access token and per-phone cookies — use it only on a trusted network and never forward port 8765 from a router. The old `env.download` Vercel token is removed from the tree but remains in Git history — revoke it in Vercel.
